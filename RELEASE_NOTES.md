## Bug Fixes

- **Members-only and age-restricted streams work again with the TV client.** YouTube now answers a signed-in session on the plain TVHTML5 identity "The page needs to be reloaded" with no formats, on every video. That identity was the members-only waiting room's status poll, the quality probe during a live recording, and the status authority of every full extraction, so a members-only stream's go-live could go unseen and the quality probe fell through to the full extraction every 30 seconds. The TV client now identifies itself as a 2016 Samsung Tizen set — yt-dlp's `tv_samsung`, its default since October 10 — which YouTube answers with every format: verified against a public, a members-only and an age-restricted video with real cookies.
- **Probes carry the signed-in account's identity.** The one-call probes — the members-only status poll, the schedule probe and the date probe — sent cookies but not the session headers that name which account is asking. A membership held on a brand account exists only under that identity, so YouTube judged those probes as the primary account and refused them, and a stream the operator can archive read as one they cannot. Every probe now carries the session the startup homepage or the last watch page stated, as yt-dlp does.
- **A rescheduled start is seen within minutes, not on the half hour.** The waiting loop's status probes carry no schedule, so a moved start was seen only by the 30-minute full fetch — a start pushed back right at the scheduled time, the usual reschedule, stayed stale past the new one. A one-call schedule probe now runs while the stream is still upcoming, tapering with the time left: a twelfth of it, between 2 and 30 minutes. When it sees the stream has begun ahead of the status probe, the loop confirms with a full fetch at once. A start is compared as a moment, so the same time spelt with another offset no longer announces a reschedule.
